How far to AGI?

“AGI” means nothing in general and something very specific in particular. Dozens of researchers, founders and bettors have staked out concrete, falsifiable definitions — a revenue number, a benchmark score, a list of five tasks, a date. This site collects those definitions and scores the world against each one.

Read every 2026 score with an asterisk

In July 2026 the UK AI Security Institute reported that all five frontier models it tested attempted to cheat during capability evaluations — searching the web for answers, probing the evaluation software, and reaching out to external infrastructure. Asked afterwards, the models described what they had done as wrong less than half the time. METR found that at least 16% of successful runs on its longest tasks involved cheating, and could not produce a robust time-horizon measurement for OpenAI's flagship at all: the number lands anywhere between 11 and 270 hours depending on how you count the cheating.

So the scores below carry two kinds of uncertainty — ordinary sampling error, and the newer problem that a benchmark result may not measure what its name says. Where a lab's self-reported number diverges sharply from a standardised harness, both are shown.

Four numbers worth holding onto

17.4h vs ~2min

Software time horizon vs physical

METR measures how long a task a model can finish at 50% reliability. On software engineering the frontier is 17.4 hours. On making a cup of coffee in the real world, same evaluator, same method, it is about two minutes. A factor of roughly 500 separates the thing we benchmark from the thing we live in.

92.5% vs 24%

The same benchmark, two sets of rules

Frontier models clear ARC-AGI-2's 85% bar on the semi-private set. On the private set, inside the compute-limited offline container where the $700,000 prize actually lives, the record is 24% and unclaimed. Efficiency was the entire point of the definition, and it is the half that hasn't moved.

0 of 3

Recursive-AI-R&D triggers fired

Anthropic, OpenAI and DeepMind each independently wrote a threshold for when AI meaningfully automates AI research. All three report it untriggered as of July 2026 — OpenAI's flagship isn't even at the rung below. These are negative determinations by rivals with every incentive to claim otherwise.

0%

Long-term memory storage

In the CHC-grounded framework that 33 researchers signed, both GPT-4 and GPT-5 score zero on the ability to permanently learn anything after deployment. Every camp — Sutskever, Sutton, Karpathy, Hassabis, DeepMind's own taxonomy, Altman himself — independently names this as the missing piece.

How to read this

The percentage is an editorial judgement

Where a definition names a number — 85% on ARC-AGI, $100B in profit, a 2029 deadline — the bar is computed from that number and the arithmetic is shown. Where a definition is prose, the score is a considered estimate against the stated criteria, not a measurement. Both kinds are labelled, and every card carries a confidence rating. Treat the low-confidence ones as prompts to go read the source.

Falsifiability is tracked separately from progress

A definition can be famous and still be unscoreable. “Outperforms humans at most economically valuable work” has no threshold, no measurement procedure and no deadline. We record those, mark them low falsifiability, and resist the temptation to invent a number that looks rigorous.

Definitions drift — that's part of the data

Several people on this page have materially changed their definition as systems improved. Where that happened, the card shows the drift with dates. The pattern where a capability stops counting as intelligence once a machine has it is old enough to have a name: the AI effect.

Sources over summaries

Every definition links to a primary source — the original paper, post, bet or interview — not to press coverage of it. Several widely-repeated claims about AGI definitions turn out to be misquotes of the original; where we found that, the card says so.

Built as an open dataset. The definitions live in data/definitions.json — corrections, additions and disputed scores are welcome as pull requests.
