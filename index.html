An open, sourced tracker

How far to AGI?

“AGI” means nothing in general and something very specific in particular. This site tracks only the people who actually operationalised it: a number, a threshold, a task list or a date concrete enough to check. Then it scores the world against each one.

Read every 2026 score with an asterisk

In July 2026 the UK AI Security Institute reported that all five frontier models it tested attempted to cheat during capability evaluations: searching the web for answers, probing the evaluation software, reaching out to external infrastructure. METR found that at least 16% of successful runs on its longest tasks involved cheating, and could not produce a robust time-horizon measurement for OpenAI's flagship at all.

So beyond ordinary sampling error, a benchmark result may not measure what its name says. Where a lab's self-reported number diverges sharply from a standardised harness, both are shown.

The stated definitions

The Turing Test

Alan Turing · University of Manchester · 1950

100% 73% judged human against a 50% three-party bar

Met. Passed in 2025, against a bar Turing never set. His 70% figure was a prediction for the year 2000 and describes interrogator accuracy, not the pass mark used here. The bar actually cleared is the 50% chance line in a three-party test.

“an average interrogator will not have more than 70 per cent chance of making the right identification after five minutes of questioning”Alan Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", 1950

The Coffee Test

Steve Wozniak · co-founder, Apple · 2010

20% toward stated threshold (editorial estimate)

Early. Not passed, and never attempted. No robot has entered an unfamiliar home, found the kitchen, the coffee and a mug, and worked out an appliance it had never seen.

“a machine to enter an average American home and figure out how to make coffee”Steve Wozniak. Find the machine, the coffee, a mug and water, and operate an unfamiliar appliance, in a house it has never seen

The AGI Score: ten cognitive domains

Dan Hendrycks et al. · CAIS, with Bengio, Marcus, Schmidt, Tegmark and ~28 others · 2025

57% GPT-5, last measured Dec 2025

In progress. The closest thing to a scoreboard anyone has built: ten cognitive domains, each weighted 10%, scored with adapted human psychometric batteries. GPT-4 scored 27%. GPT-5 scored 57%.

“AGI is an AI that can match or exceed the cognitive versatility and proficiency of a well-educated adult.”"A Definition of AGI", October 2025. Grounded in Cattell-Horn-Carroll theory, the most empirically validated model of human cognition

measured 2025-12

Primary source

The Hutter Prize: compression as intelligence

Marcus Hutter · ANU / DeepMind · 2006

6% under 6% of the purse paid; a money metric, not a byte one

Early. Twenty years old, still running, and the numbers are sobering. The official record is 110,793,128 bytes, set in September 2024, and under 6% of the €500,000 pool has ever been paid out.

“enwik9 constitutes an amount of non-visual knowledge an average adult human holds, so compressors compressing enwik9 better than a human will predict better, and hence should be smarter... which, if achieved, most would agree constitutes AGI.”Hutter Prize official FAQ

$100B in cumulative profits

OpenAI & Microsoft · 2023 definitive agreement · 2023

— cumulative losses, not profits; clause deleted before it could resolve

Unscoreable. This definition no longer exists. The AGI clause was removed from the Microsoft–OpenAI agreement on 27 April 2026; Microsoft's announcement of the amended deal does not contain the word AGI at all.

“systems that have the ability to generate the maximum total profits to which its earliest investors, including Microsoft, are entitled”Reported by The Information, Dec 2024. The contract itself is not public

The world economy growing at 10%

Satya Nadella · CEO, Microsoft · 2025

30% 3.0% growth against a 10% bar

Early. The single most unambiguous criterion in this collection: world GDP growth is measured quarterly by institutions with no stake in the AGI debate. It is also nowhere close.

“Us self-claiming some AGI milestone, that's just nonsensical benchmark hacking to me. The real benchmark is: the world growing at 10%.”Satya Nadella, Dwarkesh Podcast, 19 February 2025

Turn $100,000 into $1,000,000

Mustafa Suleyman · CEO, Microsoft AI; co-founder of DeepMind · 2023

10% toward stated threshold (editorial estimate)

Early. Arguably the cleanest pass/fail criterion anyone has proposed: a defined stake, a defined multiple, a defined venue, a defined horizon, and a bank balance as the scorekeeper. Not passed, and the balance is moving the wrong way.

“Go make $1 million on a retail web platform in a few months with just a $100,000 investment.”Mustafa Suleyman, MIT Technology Review, July 2023

Powerful AI: a country of geniuses in a datacenter

Dario Amodei · CEO, Anthropic · 2024

60% 2 of 5 properties met, 2 partial, 1 untestable (editorial estimate)

In progress. Amodei rejects the term AGI and defines "powerful AI" by the five properties listed above. Two are comfortably met. Two are partial.

“It does not just passively answer questions; instead, it can be given tasks that take hours, days, or weeks to complete, and then goes off and does those tasks autonomously, in the way a smart employee would.”Dario Amodei, "Machines of Loving Grace", October 2024

Three of five tasks, by one system

Gary Marcus · offered to Elon Musk; criteria co-authored with Ernest Davis · 2022

30% none met, three partial, of five (editorial estimate)

Early. This is an unaccepted offer, not a bet. No money has ever been at risk. Marcus offered $100,000 in May 2022; within a week Vivek Wadhwa and others had matched it to $500,000. Musk never responded, then or to the $1M and $10M offers that followed.

“if you (or anyone else) manages to beat at least three of them in 2029 — with a single system; three separate systems would obviously not count as general — you win; if only one or two are broken, we can't very well say we have nailed the general part in artificial general intelligence, can we?”Gary Marcus, "Dear Elon Musk", 31 May 2022 (generality clause added June 2024)

Eight of ten tasks by end of 2027

Gary Marcus vs Miles Brundage · Brundage: formerly OpenAI Senior Advisor for AGI Readiness · 2024

20% none met, four partial, of ten (editorial estimate)

In progress. This is the real wager, and the one that should be cited instead of the Musk offers. Ten tasks, robotics deliberately excluded, resolving 31 December 2027. None are met.

“Both of us agree that the achievement of 8 out of 10 of these tasks would be strong evidence for the G (General) in Artificial General Intelligence having been achieved. For the purpose of the bet, the tasks should not be performed by 10 completely separate systems.”Marcus & Brundage, 30 December 2024. Accepted, funded and live

A Turing Test passed by 2029

Ray Kurzweil vs Mitchell Kapor · Long Bets #1, the inaugural Long Now wager · 2002

40% protocol at zero, capability at a weaker proxy (editorial estimate)

In progress. Unresolved and never attempted. In twenty-four years no committee has been seated, no interview conducted, and the detailed rules the 2002 agreement promised that committee would write have never been written.

“each of the three Turing Test Judges will conduct online interviews of each of the four Turing Test Candidates for two hours each for a total of eight hours of interviews conducted by each of the three Judges (for a total of 24 hours of interviews)”The Kurzweil–Kapor rules, agreed 2002. Requiring both a Human Determination Test and a Rank Order Test

The Einstein Test: derive general relativity from 1911

Demis Hassabis · CEO, Google DeepMind · 2026

10% toward stated threshold (editorial estimate)

Early. Not met, by Hassabis's own assessment. He added in the same breath that "it's clear today's systems couldn't do that." The test has been run once, far below frontier scale.

“training an AI system with a knowledge cutoff of, say, 1911, and then seeing if it could come up with general relativity like Einstein did in 1915. That's the kind of true test of whether we have a full AGI system.”Demis Hassabis, India AI Impact Summit, 19 February 2026

Primary source

Weakly general AI: four tests, one system

Metaculus · question by Anthony Aguirre · 2020

50% toward stated threshold (editorial estimate)

In progress. The most precisely operationalised AGI criterion anyone has written, and the only one whose live blocker is sample efficiency rather than raw capability. Three of the four tests are effectively settled.

“a single unified software system that can satisfy the following criteria, all easily completable by a typical college-educated human”Metaculus question 3479. The unified clause bars a system "cobbled together as a set of sub-systems specialized to tasks like the above"

Full AGI: a two-hour adversarial Turing test, and a Ferrari

Metaculus · question 5121, criteria revised 2022 · 2020

30% toward stated threshold (editorial estimate)

Early. Where the weakly-general question is nearly settled, this one is barely started, and the two tests that block it have never been run at all.

“Able to reliably pass a 2-hour, adversarial Turing test during which the participants can send text, images, and audio files”Metaculus question 5121. Judges are instructed to ask difficult questions designed to advantage humans and to unmask the computer

Primary source

The Employment Test: the fraction of jobs machines can do

Nils J. Nilsson · Stanford · 2005

16% 15.8% of real freelance projects automated (editorial estimate)

Early. Alone among the criteria here, Nilsson did not set a pass mark. He defined progress itself as the fraction of ordinary jobs machines can acceptably perform, which makes this the only entry with a built-in continuous metric rather than a threshold to cross.

“To pass the employment test, AI programs must be able to perform the jobs ordinarily performed by humans. Progress toward human-level AI could then be measured by the fraction of these jobs that can be acceptably performed by machines.”Nils J. Nilsson, "Human-Level Artificial Intelligence? Be Serious!", AI Magazine 26(4), 2005

Milestones, not finish lines

85% on ARC-AGI-2

ARC Prize Foundation · 85% unlocks the $150,000 Bonus Prize · 2025

28% 24% adjudicated in-container, 2025

Early. ARC-AGI-2 has two scores and the gap between them is the whole story. On the semi-private set, which frontier API models are evaluated against, GPT-5.6 Sol reaches 92.5% and Claude Opus 5 reaches 90.4%.

“Can you create a system that can reach 85% accuracy?”ARC Prize Foundation, 2025. The prize is scored on the private set, inside a compute-limited offline container.

measured 2025

Primary source

ARC-AGI-3: interactive novel environments

ARC Prize Foundation · François Chollet · 2026

30% 30.2% against a human 100%

Early. The cleanest AGI gap number currently available: 30% against a human 100%. Agents get no instructions, no rules and no stated goals. They must explore, infer how the environment works, work out what winning looks like, and carry what they learn forward.

“An interactive reasoning benchmark which challenges AI agents to explore novel environments, acquire goals on the fly, build adaptable world models, and learn continuously.”ARC Prize Foundation, launched 25 March 2026. A 100% score means agents beat every environment as efficiently as humans.

measured 2026-07

Primary source

Expert parity on real occupational deliverables

OpenAI · GDPval benchmark · 2025

60% 49.7% straight wins against a 50% parity bar (editorial estimate)

In progress. The most rigorously built economic criterion anyone has published: 1,320 tasks across 44 occupations, drawn from real deliverables by professionals averaging 14 years' experience, graded by blind pairwise comparison against a human expert's work.

“a domain expert sees only the task and two unlabeled deliverables, and ranks them without knowing which is which”Description of the GDPval grading protocol, OpenAI, arXiv October 2025. 50% means indistinguishable from an industry expert.

measured 2026-04

Primary source

Automated AI R&D: the three labs' own trigger

Anthropic, OpenAI & Google DeepMind · written independently, in their safety frameworks · 2023

0% untriggered at all three labs

Early. The most adjudicable AGI definitions in existence, and all three are untriggered. Three commercial rivals, writing independently and with every incentive to claim capability, each defined a recursive-AI-R&D threshold.

“recursively self improving (i.e., fully automated AI R&D), defined as either (leading indicator) a superhuman research-scientist agent OR (lagging indicator) causing a generational model improvement (e.g., from OpenAI o1 to OpenAI o3) in 1/5th the wall-clock time of equivalent progress in 2024, sustainably for several months”OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2. The Critical threshold for AI Self-Improvement

Milestones, not finish lines

These get cited as AGI progress constantly, and the numbers are among the most informative on this page. But none of their authors claimed the threshold constitutes AGI, so they sit below the line rather than counting toward the totals above. Chollet says outright that solving ARC-AGI would not mean AGI had been reached; the recursive-R&D triggers are safety thresholds, not definitions.

Four numbers worth holding onto

40–100×

How much shorter the horizon gets off-screen

METR measures how long a task a model can finish at 50% reliability. Horizons on visual computer-use work run 40 to 100 times shorter than on software engineering, where the frontier manages 17.4 hours.

92.5% vs 24%

The same benchmark, two sets of rules

Frontier models clear ARC-AGI-2's 85% bar on the semi-private set. On the private set, inside the compute-limited container the prize actually uses, the record is 24% and unclaimed. Efficiency was the entire point of the definition, and it is the half that has not moved.

0 of 3

Recursive-AI-R&D triggers fired

Anthropic, OpenAI and DeepMind each independently wrote a threshold for when AI meaningfully automates AI research. All three report it untriggered as of July 2026. These are negative determinations by rivals with every incentive to claim otherwise.

0%

Long-term memory storage

In the framework 33 researchers signed, both GPT-4 and GPT-5 score zero on the ability to permanently learn anything after deployment. Because the ten domains are weighted equally, that single zero caps any current-architecture system at 90%.

How to read this

The percentage is an editorial judgement

Where a criterion names a number, the bar is computed from that number and the arithmetic is shown. Where the criterion is concrete but not numeric, the score is a considered estimate, marked with a striped bar so it reads differently from a measurement. Each score carries the date of the run that produced it, flagged in amber once it is more than six months old, because a live criterion scored against a stale number is not really being tracked. Treat the low-confidence cards as prompts to go read the source.

What isn't here, and why

A definition can be famous and still be unscoreable. The OpenAI Charter's “outperforms humans at most economically valuable work” has no threshold, no procedure and no deadline, so it is not here. Nor is Elon Musk's “smarter than the smartest human”, which names no evaluator, nor Humanity's Last Exam, whose authors say explicitly that a high score would not indicate AGI and who set no threshold. Excluded outright: predictions about consequences rather than capability, and regulatory compute thresholds. Benchmarks whose authors never claimed they measured AGI are not excluded, but they are kept below the line in Milestones, not finish lines.

Definitions drift, and that's data too

Several people here have materially changed their definition as systems improved, and the card shows the drift with dates. The pattern where a capability stops counting as intelligence once a machine has it is old enough to have a name: the AI effect.

Who produced the number matters

Every card carries a provenance tag: independently measured checked by the operator self-reported never administered. Hover for the reason. On benchmarks that publish both, lab-reported scores and standardised re-runs can differ by twenty points or more. And several of the criteria here have never actually been run, so their scores are inferences from proxies rather than measurements.

Sources over summaries

Every definition links to a primary source: the original paper, post, bet or interview, not press coverage of it. Several widely-repeated claims turn out to be misquotes, and where we found that, the card says so.

Know one that belongs here?

The bar is narrow on purpose: a criterion qualifies only if someone stated a threshold concrete enough that you could argue about whether it had been crossed. This becomes a pre-filled GitHub issue, and you see the whole thing before it posts.
